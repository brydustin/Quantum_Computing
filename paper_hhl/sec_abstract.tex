\begin{abstract}
The algorithm of Harrow, Hassidim and Lloyd (HHL) prepares a quantum state proportional to the
solution of a linear system $Ax=b$, and it is the prototype of quantum linear algebra. We present a
machine-checked verification of HHL in Isabelle/HOL that spans the whole algorithm: from the
spectral theorem, through phase estimation and the error analysis of the original paper, to programs
of quantum Hoare logic and, finally, circuits of one- and two-qubit gates. For eigenvalues on the
phase-estimation grid we prove total correctness of the repeat-until-success program, which
terminates with probability one in the normalised solution after $1/p$ rounds on average. For
arbitrary spectra we prove the paper's Theorem~1 with explicit constants, and for the shifted cycle
Laplacian we compile the entire algorithm, including state preparation and Hamiltonian simulation, to
elementary gates with an explicit polynomial gate count. Mechanising the paper's appendix refuted
seven of its statements, among them the constant of its Lemma~3, which must be $\pi^2$ rather than
$\pi^2/2$.

The development has 130 theories and 64{,}000 lines. An AI coding agent wrote almost all of it,
directed by one mathematician, during 34 active days spread over six months that include a pause
of almost five months. HHL itself, on top of a formalisation of a lecture course on quantum
computing, took eleven days. We report how the work was organised, what the human did and what the
agent did, how the agent failed, and what we learned about HHL and about formalising with an agent.
\keywords{Isabelle/HOL \and HHL algorithm \and Quantum Hoare logic \and Phase estimation \and
AI-assisted formalisation \and Formal verification}
\end{abstract}
