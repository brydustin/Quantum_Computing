\section{Down to Elementary Gates}\label{sec:gates}

The programs above take $U_b$, $\Lambda(e^{iAt_0})$ and the controlled rotation as unitaries. An
algorithm whose cost is measured in gates should not. A matrix is \emph{elementary} if it is a one- or
two-qubit unitary acting on adjacent wires, padded with identities:

%%LST Algorithms/HHL_Gates.thy elementary
\begin{lstlisting}[float=tp,caption={Elementary gates},label={lst:elem}]
definition elementary :: "nat ~$\Rightarrow$~ complex Matrix.mat ~$\Rightarrow$~ bool" where
  "elementary K U ~$\longleftrightarrow$~ (~$\exists$~a b q G. q ~$\in$~ {1, 2} ~$\wedge$~ G ~$\in$~ carrier_mat (2 ^ q)
      (2 ^ q) ~$\wedge$~ Quantum.unitary G ~$\wedge$~ 0 < a ~$\wedge$~ 0 < b ~$\wedge$~ K = a * (2 ^ q *
      b) ~$\wedge$~ U = 1~$_{m}$~ a ~$\otimes$~ (G ~$\otimes$~ 1~$_{m}$~ b))"
\end{lstlisting}

Refining the programs gate by gate, we replace $H^{\otimes k}$ by $k$ Hadamard gates, $F$ by the
AFP's Fourier circuit, the clock-controlled rotation by a uniformly controlled rotation with
Walsh--Hadamard angles and parity chains of nearest-neighbour CNOTs, $U_b$ by $k$ levels of
controlled rotations for an arbitrary unit~$b$, and, for $s$-sparse $A$, $e^{iAt}$ by a Trotter
circuit over a colouring of the matrix into $2s-1$ one-sparse parts, with error $8(sLt)^2/r$ after
$r(6s-2)$ oracle queries. What remains are oracles: for the entries of $A$, for the angles of $b$,
and for the sparsity pattern.


\subsection{Sparse Hamiltonian Simulation}\label{sec:sparse}

The paper assumes an $s$-sparse $A$ and cites simulation results for $e^{iAt}$~\cite{berry2007}. We verify
the first-order construction in three steps. The Lie--Trotter bound of Section~\ref{sec:math} reduces the
problem to simulating parts. The graph of $A$ is edge-coloured greedily with $2s-1$ colours; each colour
class is a matching, given by an involution $q_c$ of the indices, so $A$ is its diagonal plus $2s-1$
Hermitian one-sparse matrices whose entries are entries of~$A$ (\isa{sparse\_split})~\cite{aharonov2003}.
Each one-sparse evolution is then implemented exactly on $\ket x\ket y\ket b$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sparse}): a
colour oracle $O_c$ writes the partner $q_c(x)$, a comparator sorts the pair, an entry gate rotates the
sign qubit by $e^{id\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&a\\\bar a&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)}$ with $a=A_{xy}$, and the
comparator and $O_c^\dagger$ uncompute.

\begin{theorem}[sparse simulation]\label{thm:sparse}
Let $A$ be Hermitian with entries bounded by $L$, and let $p$ colour its graph with $2s-1$ matchings,
given by colour oracles. Then $r$ Trotter steps of the circuit of Fig.~\ref{fig:sparse} act on
$\ket x\ket0\ket0$ as a unitary $V$ with $\norm{Vv-e^{iAt}v}\le8(sLt)^2\norm v/r$, and they make
$r(6s-2)$ oracle queries.\isalink{Algorithms/HHL_Sparse_Circuit.thy}{915}
\end{theorem}

%%LST Algorithms/HHL_Sparse_Circuit.thy sparse_simulation_circuit
\begin{lstlisting}[float=tp,caption={Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse}: the gate list \isa{sim\_gates} runs as \isa{sim\_mat}, which is $8(sLt)^2/r$-close to the evolution, with $r(6s-2)$ queries},label={lst:sparse}]
theorem sparse_simulation_circuit:
  assumes A: "A ~$\in$~ carrier_mat n n" "hermitian A" and s: "1 ~$\le$~ s"
    and L: "0 ~$\le$~ L" and Lb: "~$\bigwedge$~i j. i < n ~$\Longrightarrow$~ j < n ~$\Longrightarrow$~ cmod (A ~\$\$~ (i,
        j)) ~$\le$~ L"
    and cov: "edge_cover n (2 * s - 1) p (edges n A)"
    and Os: "~$\bigwedge$~c. c < 2 * s - 1 ~$\Longrightarrow$~ colour_oracle n (p c) (Os c)" and r:
        "0 < r"
  shows "sg_run n (t / real r) A Os (sim_gates (2 * s - 1) r) * emb n =
      emb n * sim_mat n (t / real r) A (2 * s - 1) p r"
    and "sim_mat n (t / real r) A (2 * s - 1) p r ~$\in$~ carrier_mat n n"
    and "unitary (sim_mat n (t / real r) A (2 * s - 1) p r)"
    and "sim_mat n (t / real r) A (2 * s - 1) p r
        ~\closeto{8 * (real s * L * t)\textsuperscript{2} / real r}~
        evolution A t"
    and "length (filter is_query (sim_gates (2 * s - 1) r)) = r * (6 * s
        - 2)"
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Compiling the Oracles}\label{sec:lap}

 The last step removes them for a concrete family. A reversible
comparator on clean ancillas, built from Toffoli and CNOT gates and routed to adjacent wires, computes
the colours and entries of a banded matrix; sparse right-hand sides are prepared from a lookup table
without any angle oracle; and the cycle Laplacian is simulated by a Trotter product whose every gate
is elementary.

\begin{theorem}[HHL for $L+\mu I$ in elementary gates]\label{thm:lap}
Let $L$ be the Laplacian of the cycle on $2^m$ vertices, $\mu\ge0$, and $b$ a unit vector with
nonnegative entries supported on a list $xs$. For clock size $2^k$, $r$ Trotter steps, angles with
$\mathit{bb}$ bits and $a$ ancillas, an explicit list of gates on $K=m+k+1+a$ qubits satisfies: every
gate is elementary; applied to $\ket0$ it computes the HHL output for a right-hand side $b'$ with
$\norm{b'-b}\le m\cdot2\pi/2^{\mathit{bb}}$; the flag reads $1$ with amplitude at least $Ct_0/\pi$;
the post-selected state is within $2\delta/(C\norm x)$ of $\psi\otimes x/\norm x\otimes\ket1$ for
$x=(L+\mu I)^{-1}b'$ and $\delta=\epsilon+2(2^k-1)\eta$; and the number of gates is at most an explicit
polynomial in $m$, $k$, $r$, $a$, $\mathit{bb}$, $|xs|$ and the clock size $2^k$.\isalink{Algorithms/Gates/HHL_Laplacian_Sparse.thy}{77}
\end{theorem}

The count is dominated by the $2(2^k-1)$ controlled Trotter products of the two phase estimations,
each with $r$ steps of $O(m^2K)$ gates. The error budget forces $r$ to grow with the clock size: the
hypothesis $\epsilon+2(2^k-1)\eta\le Ct_0/\pi$ with $\eta=18(2+\mu)^2t_0^2/r$ needs
$r=\Omega(2^k(2+\mu)^2t_0/C)$. The total is therefore $O\bigl(4^k(2+\mu)^2t_0\,m^2K/C\bigr)$ plus
lower-order terms: polynomial in the number $m$ of system qubits, that is polylogarithmic in the
dimension $2^m$, and quadratic in the clock size $2^k$. HHL's analysis charges each call of $e^{iAt_0}$
the cost of a higher-order simulation and obtains a better dependence on the clock size; with
first-order Trotter we pay a further factor of it. The oracle-free result is for this one family; for
general sparse $A$ the oracles of Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse} remain. Figure~\ref{fig:budget} shows how the
three sources of error combine. The theorem is the end point of the stack: it starts from a sparse vector of real numbers and
ends with a list of two-qubit gates whose output is provably close to the normalised solution.

\subsection{What Is Trusted}\label{sec:trust}

Isabelle2026 checks the development with \texttt{quick\_and\_dirty = false} against the AFP release
of 29~October~2026. Theorems may still depend on oracles, extensions of the logic that Isabelle
records rather than proves. A check over every theorem with \isa{Thm\_Deps.all\_oracles} finds one
oracle, \isa{cancel\_type\_definition} of Types-To-Sets, in exactly two side sessions: the search lower
bound behind the paper's optimality theorem, imported from \emph{Compressed
Oracles}~\cite{unruh2025co}, and the bridge to \emph{Kraus Maps}, whose trace-class type depends on
it. The main sessions are oracle-free. The specification is trusted in the usual sense: a reader must
check that the definitions of Listings~\ref{lst:triples}--\ref{lst:lap} say what the informal theorems
say. We return to this in Section~\ref{sec:lessons}, because it is the one part of the work that the
agent could not do for us.
