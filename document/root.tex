\begin{abstract}
This document is generated by Isabelle from the project theories.  Every
definition and theorem shown here has been checked by Isabelle/HOL (proofs are
folded).  The commentary restates Paulsen's lecture notes lecture by lecture
(Day~1 = 22~August~2011, Day~34 = 18~November~2011): definitions and results in
the notes' own language, each followed by the formal statement.  The informal
proofs of the notes are omitted; the formal proofs replace them.  Where the
formalisation deviates from the notes, the commentary says so.  Background matrix facts that the lectures use but do not teach are collected in
a separate prerequisite session, \emph{Matrix Analysis Prerequisites}, with its own document.  A companion entry builds on it: quantum phase estimation with the HHL algorithm.
\end{abstract}

\appendix
\section{What is not formalised}

Every definition, theorem and worked example of the notes is formalised, with the following
exceptions.

\subsection*{Left out}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Narrative and asides.}  The course overview, history and references (Day~1); the
  classical gates AND, OR, XOR, NAND, NOR and the Peirce/Sheffer remarks (Day~10); the
  Bernoulli-trial remark (Day~12); stochastic matrices and Markov chains (Day~17); left inverses
  and cyclic codes (Day~29).
\item \emph{Abstract Hilbert spaces} (Day~1).  Every space in the notes is $\C^n$ or
  $M_{n,k}$, so the abstract definition is never needed.  The identification
  $M_{n,k}\cong\C^{nk}$ is used but not stated as a theorem.
\item \emph{Motivation for density matrices} (Day~13): that a vector in
  $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_E$ behaves like an ensemble.  Its rigorous form is the
  partial-trace theorem of Day~15, which is formalised.
\item \emph{Remarks.}  That a density matrix has many factorisations $\rho=XX^*$ (Day~14); that
  the lemma ``$\langle x|Tx\rangle=0$ for all $x$ implies $T=0$'' fails over $\mathbb R$ (Day~4);
  the rank-one lemma for $(E_{ij})$ (Day~20) and ``$T$ injective implies $T^*$ onto''
  (Day~21), which the formal proofs do not need.
\item \emph{The Krein--Milman theorem} (Day~22), which the notes only state; it is available
  in HOL-Analysis and is not restated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Formalised in a different form}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Cholesky reduction} (Day~5) carries the hypothesis $p_{11}>0$, which the notes leave
  implicit; without it the statement is false.
\item \emph{Hadamard gate} (Day~10): the notes state $H^2=X$ and correct it on Day~11; the
  formalisation proves $H^2=I$.
\item \emph{The converse of Choi's Theorem~3} (Day~23) is proved through a CP
  Radon--Nikodym theorem instead of Choi's second theorem.
\item \emph{The example $\tfrac12(U_1^*XU_1+U_2^*XU_2)$} (Day~23) is not extreme only when
  $U_1,U_2$ are linearly independent; for proportional $U_1,U_2$ the map is extreme, and both
  cases are proved.
\item \emph{Arveson's extension theorem} (Day~26) is proved by extending the associated
  functional to a positive functional on $M_d(M_n)$ directly, by separating the positive
  definite cone from its kernel.  The norm proposition of Day~25 and the reduction to the
  unital case of Day~26 are therefore not needed.
\item \emph{Images of the one-qubit Pauli errors} (Day~32): the notes' claim that they are
  mutually orthogonal is false as stated ($Z_1\neq Z_2$ have the same image of the code word);
  the notes correct it on Day~33, and the formalisation proves the counterexample and the
  corrected statement.
\end{itemize}
