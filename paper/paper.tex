\begin{abstract}
We report on a formalisation in Isabelle/HOL of the mathematical content of Vern
Paulsen's 2011 lecture course \emph{Quantum Computing and Completely Positive Maps}
(34 lectures): finite-dimensional Hilbert space theory, the postulates of quantum
mechanics for pure and mixed states, partial traces, completely positive maps with
Choi's theorem and the Choi--Kraus representation, the extreme points of the sets of
unital and trace-preserving completely positive maps (Choi's Theorem~3), the
Hahn--Banach theorem on matrix spaces, and the separating-hyperplane theory of
entanglement witnesses.  The development comprises forty-eight theories and about a
thousand checked statements on top of the Jordan-Normal-Form and \IMD libraries; it
builds without \texttt{sorry} or axioms and passes an oracle audit.  A lecture-by-lecture
audit records, for each statement in the notes, its formal counterpart or the reason
it is absent, and an Isabelle-generated document reproduces the notes with the formal
statements in place of the informal proofs.  We then ask what this body of verified
mathematics contributes towards a machine-checked proof of the Harrow--Hassidim--Lloyd
(HHL) algorithm in Isabelle, and answer it with a sixth part beyond the notes: a
functional calculus for Hermitian matrices, quantum phase estimation (exact case and the
$4/\pi^2$ success bound, on the Archive's QFT circuit) and the HHL algorithm for
eigenvalues representable in the register, with the output state, the solution
property, a $1/\kappa^2$ success bound and the reduction of non-Hermitian systems.  The
for an eigenvalue that is not representable, the output of the physical (angle-clamped)
circuit is within an explicit $O(1/(t_0\sqrt N))$ of the exact-HHL output, for every
right-hand side.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Machine-checked quantum computing in Isabelle/HOL has so far concentrated on
algorithms: \IMD~\cite{bordg2021imd,bordg2020afp} formalises qubits, gates, the
no-cloning theorem, teleportation and the Deutsch and Deutsch--Jozsa algorithms on
concrete matrices; the quantum Hoare logic of Liu et al.~\cite{liu2019qhl,liu2019afp}
verifies Grover's algorithm; Manrique~\cite{manrique2025qft} adds the quantum Fourier
transform.  The mathematics that a graduate course on the subject spends most of its
time on---positive matrices, the density-matrix formulation, partial traces,
completely positive maps and their Choi matrices, the convex geometry of channels and
of separable states---is covered unevenly.  Unruh's libraries~\cite{caballero2021cbo,
unruh2024tensor,unruh2025kraus} develop much of this for arbitrary Hilbert spaces, in a
type-based representation of operators; Echenim's entry~\cite{echenim2021pm}
formalises projective measurements.

This paper describes a different kind of project: the end-to-end formalisation of one
set of lecture notes, Paulsen's \emph{Lectures on Quantum Computing and Completely
Positive Maps} (Houston, Fall 2011)~\cite{paulsen2011notes}, taken as the specification.
The notes are an unedited transcription of 34 lectures in five parts (basic
mathematics; introduction to quantum computing; the theory of completely positive maps;
entanglement; error correction).  Following them lecture by lecture forces one to
formalise the whole supporting theory rather than the shortest path to a headline
theorem, and it produces a natural, externally fixed measure of completeness: the audit
of Section~\ref{sec:audit}.

The project started as an autoformalisation experiment and was finished by the author
with AI assistance; everything reported here is checked by Isabelle2025-2 with the
strict build option \texttt{quick\_and\_dirty = false}, and the principal theorems have
been checked to depend on no oracles.  The sources, the audit, and the generated
document are available in the project repository.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/brydustin/Quantum_Computing}}

Contributions.  (1)~A verified formalisation of Parts I--IV of the notes and of the
elementary part of Part~V: in particular Schur's theorem, the spectral theorem for
Hermitian matrices, four characterisations of positivity, Cholesky's reduction, the
measurement postulates and POVMs, the characterisation of density matrices by
ensembles, partial traces and their compatibility with local measurements, Choi's
theorem and the Choi--Kraus representation of channels, the adjoint duality between
unital and trace-preserving maps, Choi's Theorem~3 on extreme points, the Hahn--Banach
theorem for matrix spaces, and the entanglement-witness characterisation of
separability.  (2)~A lecture-by-lecture audit that classifies every definition and
result of the notes as formalised, formalised in a variant form, not formalised, or
deliberately omitted.  (3)~A document, generated by Isabelle from the theories, that
restates the notes with the formal statements replacing the informal proofs.
(4)~A sixth part beyond the notes: phase estimation and the HHL algorithm, verified on
top of this material (Section~\ref{sec:hhl}).

\section{The notes and the formalisation method}

\paragraph{Representation.}  All objects of the notes are concrete: vectors in $\C^n$ and
matrices in $M_{m,n}$.  We use the Jordan-Normal-Form library~\cite{thiemann2015jnf}:
vectors are \isa{complex Matrix.vec} with a dimension hypothesis
\isa{dim\_vec v = n}, matrices are \isa{complex Matrix.mat} with carrier hypotheses
\isa{A $\in$ carrier\_mat m n}.  Dimensions are hypotheses rather than types, which is
how the notes treat them and which is the convention of \IMD, so that its gates,
inner product, adjoint (\isa{dagger}) and tensor products can be reused directly.  Maps
$\Phi:M_n\to M_d$ are HOL functions \isa{complex Matrix.mat $\Rightarrow$ complex
Matrix.mat} constrained only on the carrier; linearity, positivity and complete
positivity are predicates on such functions.

Two places in the notes genuinely need topology (compactness and the separation of a
point from a closed convex cone, Days 22 and 27--28).  For these we introduce a typed
wrapper \isa{('a,'m,'n) lmat} of matrices of fixed dimension, instantiate the
HOL-Analysis type classes up to \isa{euclidean\_space} and \isa{heine\_borel}, and
transport the relevant statements across the wrapper.  The carrier-predicate world and
the typed world coexist; the former carries almost all of the development, the latter
is used only where HOL-Analysis is applied.

\paragraph{Fidelity.}  Statements are formalised as close to the notes as the
representation permits.  The audit distinguishes faithful statements from
\emph{variants}; the main variants are: the predicate \isa{psd} carries Hermiticity as an
explicit conjunct (equivalent by the notes' own Theorem that $\braket{x}{Hx}\in\R$ for
all $x$ iff $H=H^*$); Cholesky's reduction adds the hypothesis $p_{11}>0$ that the notes
leave implicit; linear maps are given with explicit additivity and homogeneity
hypotheses on the carrier; and Choi's Theorem~3 is proved in its trace-preserving form
with a fixed Kraus list of linearly independent operators, see Section~\ref{sec:choi3}.
Where the notes themselves are inconsistent we formalise the correct statement and say
so in the commentary (the notes claim $H^2=X$ on Day~10 and correct it to $H^2=I$ on
Day~11; they use two sign conventions for the Pauli matrix $Y$).

\paragraph{Verification discipline.}  The project builds as three Isabelle sessions
with \texttt{quick\_and\_dirty = false}, so no \texttt{sorry} can survive.  We also
ran an oracle audit (\isa{Thm\_Deps.all\_oracles} over the principal theorems).  This
turned up a methodological point worth recording: under \texttt{quick\_and\_dirty =
true}, Isabelle's \isa{Goal.prove\_sorry} discharges package-internal goals of
\isa{fun} and \isa{record} definitions with \isa{Skip\_Proof.cheat\_tac}, so every
theorem downstream of such a definition reports the oracle \isa{Pure.skip\_proof} even
in a development that contains no \texttt{sorry} at all.  Oracle audits are only
meaningful on builds with the strict option.

\paragraph{Lectures 1--10: linear algebra and the postulates.}  Besides the inner-product
algebra, matrix units and the trace (\isa{Tr\_cyclic}, \isa{hs\_inner\_prod\_as\_trace}),
the first part proves the projection formula $P=\sum\ketbra{v_i}{v_i}$ and Parseval
(\isa{parseval\_norm}), the characterisations of unitaries, Householder unitaries and
Schur's lemma (\isa{schur\_lemma}), Schur's triangularisation (\isa{schur\_theorem}) and
$\Tr A=\sum\lambda_i$, the spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices
(\isa{hermitian\_spectral\_decomp}: $U^\dagger HU$ is a real diagonal matrix), and the
positivity theory: $P\ge0$ iff Hermitian with non-negative eigenvalues
(\isa{psd\_iff\_nonneg\_eigenvalues}), iff $P=\sum\ketbra{v_i}{v_i}$
(\isa{psd\_kraus\_decomp}, \isa{outer\_sum\_psd}), positive definiteness iff the $v_i$ span
(\isa{pd\_iff\_spanning}), the positive square root, and Cholesky's reduction
(\isa{cholesky\_reduction}).  Tensor products are Kronecker products with bilinearity,
the multiplicativity of inner products and the orthonormal product basis
(\isa{tensor\_onb}).  The postulates are definitions: \isa{quantum\_state},
\isa{evolve}, \isa{measurement\_system}, \isa{meas\_prob}, with the probabilities summing
to one, POVMs with their square-root measurement systems (\isa{povm\_from\_sqrt}),
perfect distinguishability of orthogonal states, and local measurements in composite
systems (\isa{composite\_measurement\_prob}).

\paragraph{Lectures 11--22: channels and Choi's theorem.}  The no-cloning theorem
(\isa{no\_cloning}) and the existence of oracles $U_f$ (\isa{oracle\_exists}) open the
second part.  Ensembles and their density matrices are defined, and density matrices are
characterised as the positive matrices of trace one (\isa{density\_matrix\_iff\_ensemble},
via the spectral ensemble); the ensembles of a pure state consist of multiples of the
state (\isa{pure\_ensemble\_parallel}).  Partial traces are defined by the block formulas
of the notes; they are trace preserving and positivity preserving
(\isa{partial\_trace\_B\_is\_density}) and compatible with local measurements:
$\Tr((M^\dagger M\otimes I)\rho)=\Tr(M^\dagger M\,\mathrm{tr}_B\rho)$
(\isa{partial\_trace\_measurement}).  The coordinate form of
$L(\mathcal H_A)\otimes L(\mathcal H_B)\cong L(\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B)$ is an
explicit linear isomorphism.  Measurement maps $\rho\mapsto\sum M_\alpha\rho M_\alpha^\dagger$
preserve density matrices; Kraus maps are completely positive (\isa{kraus\_is\_cp}), with
complete positivity defined through the block amplification $\Phi^{(p)}$.  Choi's theorem
is \isa{choi\_theorem}: a linear $\Phi$ on the carrier is completely positive iff its Choi
matrix $(\Phi(E_{ij}))_{ij}$ is positive, and \isa{kraus\_rep\_of\_psd\_choi} extracts a
Kraus representation with $nd$ operators from a positive Choi matrix, which gives the
Choi--Kraus characterisation of channels (\isa{choi\_kraus\_cptp}) and the Kraus-rank bound
(\isa{kraus\_rank\_bound}).  The Hilbert--Schmidt adjoint of a Kraus map is the Kraus map
of the adjoint operators (\isa{kraus\_adjoint\_correct}), and $\Phi$ is unital iff $\Phi^*$ is
trace preserving (\isa{cp\_unital\_iff\_adjoint\_tp}); positive functionals are
completely positive (\isa{positive\_scalar\_is\_cp}); the sets UCP and CPTP are convex.

\paragraph{Lecture 23: Choi's Theorem 3.}\label{sec:choi3}  Choi's characterisation of the
extreme points of UCP~\cite{choi1975} is formalised in its trace-preserving form, for a
channel given by a Kraus list $V_1,\dots,V_r$ of linearly independent operators
(\isa{choi\_theorem\_3\_cptp\_iff}): $\Phi$ is extreme if and only if $\{V_j^\dagger V_i\}$ is
linearly independent over $\C$.  (The perturbation argument of the notes produces a
real-coefficient relation; a complex relation is first turned into a Hermitian one, using
that the family $\{V_j^\dagger V_i\}$ is closed under adjoints, so both directions use the
same notion of independence.)  Two aspects deserve comment.  First, because HOL functions are only
constrained on the carrier, the naive definition of an extreme point (``$\Phi$ is not the
midpoint of two \emph{different} CPTP maps'') is vacuous: any CPTP map agrees on the
carrier with maps that differ from it elsewhere.  Extremality is therefore defined through
disagreement on the carrier (\isa{extreme\_CPTP\_carrier}).  Second, the notes' proof uses
the unitary freedom of Kraus representations (their ``Choi 2''), which was formalised only
afterwards (\isa{choi\_2\_unitary\_freedom} in \isa{Day21\_Unitary\_Freedom}).  The forward
direction instead goes through a CP Radon--Nikodym theorem
(\isa{cp\_radon\_nikodym\_linear}): if $\Psi$ and $\Phi-\Psi$ are completely positive and
$\Phi(X)=\sum_iV_iXV_i^\dagger$, then $\Psi(X)=\sum_{ij}T_{ij}V_iXV_j^\dagger$ for a positive
matrix $T$.  Its proof reduces to a statement about positive matrices dominated by an
outer sum, $0\le B\le VV^\dagger$ implies $B=VTV^\dagger$ with $T\ge0$, which we prove with
the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse of $VV^\dagger$ built from the spectral theorem: the
identities $AA^+A=A$ and, for $0\le B\le A$, $AA^+B=B$ follow from the kernel inclusion
$\ker A\subseteq\ker B$, and $T=(A^+V)^\dagger B(A^+V)$.

\paragraph{Lectures 25--28: Hahn--Banach and entanglement witnesses.}  The notes state the
Hahn--Banach theorem for bounded functionals on normed spaces and use it in Arveson's
extension theorem.  We prove it for matrix spaces with the Hilbert--Schmidt norm
(\isa{matrix\_hahn\_banach\_lmat}): HOL's Hahn--Banach theorem gives a norm-preserving
real-linear extension of the real part, and the Bohnenblust--Sobczyk formula
$f(x)=F(x)-iF(ix)$ recovers a complex-linear extension with the same bound.  Arveson's
correspondence and extension theorem are formalised in the third block of
Table~\ref{tab:theories} (Section~\ref{sec:audit}).  The entanglement theory of
Days~27--28 is: the separable matrices form a closed convex cone
(\isa{separable\_cone\_lmat\_closed}, using the closedness of the positive matrices, the
compactness of the product density matrices and a Carath\'eodory bound on the number of
generators), so a non-separable $\rho$ is separated from the cone by a real functional,
which becomes a Hermitian witness $W$ with $\Re\Tr(W\rho)<0\le\Re\Tr(W\sigma)$ for all
separable $\sigma$ (\isa{entanglement\_witness\_hermitian'}); this yields the
Horodecki-type characterisation~\cite{horodecki1996}: a Hermitian $\rho$ is separable
iff every such witness is non-negative on it (\isa{separable\_iff\_witness}), with a
strict version for density matrices.  The notes phrase the criterion through positive
maps, which needs the Arveson correspondence; the witness form is the equivalent
statement that does not.

\paragraph{Lectures 29--32: error correction.}  Hamming weight and distance, the parity
check and repetition codes, the Pauli matrices and their orthogonality, the bit-flip,
phase-flip and bit-phase-flip channels (as UCP and CPTP maps), the pure-state fidelity,
and the code words of the three-qubit codes are formalised.  The recovery theorems for
the three-qubit code and the Shor code, and the Knill--Laflamme conditions of the last
lectures, are not.

\section{The audit}\label{sec:audit}

The file \texttt{NOTES\_AUDIT.md} in the repository lists, for each of the 34 lectures,
every definition, theorem, proposition, corollary, example and remark of the notes with
its formal counterpart.  Each item is classified: $\checkmark$ faithful, $\sim$ formalised
in a variant form, $\times$ not formalised, or $-$ deliberately not formalised
(overviews, history, numerical examples, classical asides such as stochastic matrices
or cyclic codes).  Of the 170 items, 10 are deliberate omissions; of the remaining 160
mathematical items, 155 are faithful, 5 are variants and none is a gap.\footnote{Counts as
of the third close-out of October 2026; the audit file is the authoritative record.}  The audit
drove a final round of proofs: small statements omitted along the way (the converses
isometry~$\Rightarrow$~unitary, positive definiteness iff positive eigenvalues, the
expected value $\braket{\psi}{H\psi}$ of an observable, the phase invariance of
$\ketbra\psi\psi$, that $\rho_1\otimes\rho_2$ is a density matrix, the unambiguous
discrimination of linearly independent states, and the complex-coefficient form of
Choi's Theorem~3) were proved during the close-out and are counted as faithful above.
A second pass closed the medium-sized gaps that the notes prove in a page, each in its
own theory (the second block of Table~\ref{tab:theories}): a linear map sends density
matrices to density matrices iff it is positive and trace preserving
(\isa{density\_preserving\_iff\_positive\_tp}); the transpose on $M_2$ is positive but not
$2$-positive, with the notes' witness $(E_{ij})$; the Hadamard power
$H^{\otimes N}\ket{0\cdots0}=2^{-N/2}\sum_J\ket J$ and the counting probability
$p_0=(M/2^N)^2$ of Day~12, for any unitary acting as the oracle on the basis; the Pauli
expansion identity $\sum|a_k|^2=1$ for unitaries; the bound $\crk(\Phi)\le n$ for extreme
CPTP maps (linear independence bounds the length, through the vector-space library of
the Jordan-Normal-Form entry); the two-qubit Horodecki--Peres criterion, $(\mathrm{id}\otimes
t)(\ketbra vv)\ge0$ iff $v$ is a product vector, with the witness value
$-2|v_0v_3-v_1v_2|^2$; and the three-qubit bit-flip code, whose syndrome projections,
CPTP recovery map and recovery theorem $R(\ketbra{\psi_i}{\psi_i})=\ketbra\psi\psi$ for at
most one flip are proved on explicit $8\times8$ matrices.  A third pass added the
structural results of lectures 20--22 and 32--34: two rank-one decompositions
$\sum v_jv_j^*=\sum w_lw_l^*$ with the $v_j$ independent are related by an isometry, and
hence two Kraus representations of one map by an isometry (Choi's Theorem~2,
\isa{choi\_2\_unitary\_freedom}, proved through the Choi matrix); a Kraus representation
of minimal length $\crk(\Phi)$ is linearly independent; the Choi rank theorem
$\crk(\Phi)=\rank P_\Phi$ (\isa{choi\_rank\_eq\_rank}, with the rank taken in the
Jordan-Normal-Form vector-space library); the general recovery proposition of Day~32,
$R(\ketbra\varphi\varphi)=(\sum_i|a_i|^2)\ketbra\psi\psi$ for $\varphi=\sum_ia_iU_i\psi$ with

between correcting the states of $V$ and fixing $PXP$, Theorem~1 in both directions
(\isa{knill\_laflamme}; necessity through Choi's Theorem~2, sufficiency by diagonalising
$(\alpha_{ij})$ and applying the recovery proposition to the rescaled mixed errors, which
need not be restrictions of unitaries) and the closure of the corrected errors under
linear combinations.  Finally the Shor code: one CPTP map corrects every single-qubit
unitary error on each of the nine qubits (\isa{shor\_code\_theorem}).  The $2^9$-dimensional
space is never handled entrywise; nine qubits are three blocks of three, each single-qubit
operator acts inside one block, inner products factor over the blocks, and the
orthogonality of the $22$ error images reduces to the distinctness of $44$ block labels,
decided by \isa{code\_simp}.  Here the formalisation found a false statement: the Day~32
proposition that distinct 1-Paulis map the code space to orthogonal subspaces fails for

$\operatorname{tr}_E(U(\rho\otimes\ketbra\varphi\varphi)U^*)=\sum_iW_i\rho W_i^*$ with
$\sum_iW_i^*W_i=I$ (\isa{environment\_model}).  Last, the operator-system part of the
course: Arveson's theorem that a linear map on an operator system is completely positive iff
it is $d$-positive iff $S_\Phi$ is positive, the correspondence $\Phi\leftrightarrow S_\Phi$,
the extension theorem (\isa{arveson\_extension}) with the Kraus form of completely positive
maps on operator systems, and the positive-map criterion for separability
(\isa{separable\_iff\_positive\_maps}).  The extension is not proved as in the notes (a state
of norm one, Hahn--Banach, and a reduction to the unital case): the positive functional
$S_\Phi$ on $M_d(S)$ is extended directly, by separating the positive definite cone from its
kernel with HOL-Analysis' separating hyperplane theorem in the Euclidean space of typed
matrices, which gives the extension $X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(DX)$ with $D\ge0$.  A fourth pass (the fourth block of Table~\ref{tab:theories}) proved the examples and small
propositions that remained: the projection does not depend on the orthonormal basis; the
unique coordinates $u=\sum_jx_j\otimes f_j$ for any basis and $X\otimes Y\cong X\oplus\cdots\oplus X$;
outcome $i$ fires on $\psi_2$ with probability at least $|\braket{\psi_1}{\psi_2}|^2$ if it is
certain on $\psi_1$; the Bell-pair measurement of Day~9; $\mathrm{SWAP}=\mathrm{CNOT}\cdot
\mathrm{CNOT}'\cdot\mathrm{CNOT}$; the classical error probabilities of Day~30; the bit-flip
code under independent flips, with $R\circ E$ and both minimum fidelities of Day~31; and the
code for $Y$ flips.  Two statements of the notes needed correction.  The Day~29 ``Hamming

cannot correct one (\isa{notes\_hamming\_not\_correcting}); the standard $[7,4,3]$ code corrects
one.  The Day~31 formula $R\circ E(Y)=c_1Y+c_2XYX$ holds for $Y$ supported on the code space,
not for a general $Y$.  The 6 remaining gaps are Sylvester's criterion (stated without proof
in the notes), the Choi-matrix form of trace preservation and unitality, two lists of
examples, and the two lemmas that only the notes' route to the extension theorem uses.

Beyond the omissions, the audit documents where the formalisation strengthens the

A fifth pass (the fifth block) proved the three items that remained: the Choi-matrix form

A sixth pass (the sixth block) closed the last three gaps.  The two Day~26 factorisations of a

Results}, whose notions of Hermitian and positive definite matrices are shown to agree with ours

\section{Relation to the Archive of Formal Proofs}

\IMD~\cite{bordg2020afp} and the quantum Fourier transform entry~\cite{manrique2025qft}
use the same matrix representation as this project (\isa{complex Matrix.mat} with

\section{A verified HHL algorithm}\label{sec:hhl}

The HHL algorithm~\cite{hhl2009} prepares, for a Hermitian $A$ with eigen-decomposition
$A=\sum_j\lambda_j\ketbra{u_j}{u_j}$ and a unit vector $\ket b=\sum_j\beta_j\ket{u_j}$, a
state proportional to $A^{-1}\ket b$: phase estimation with the unitaries $e^{iAt}$ writes
the eigenvalues into a register, a controlled rotation of an ancilla produces the
amplitudes $C/\lambda_j$, uncomputation and a measurement of the ancilla postselect the
branch $\sum_j(\beta_j/\lambda_j)\ket{u_j}$, up to the approximation error of phase
estimation and with a success probability governed by the condition number.  Machine
checked proofs exist in Coq, in the quantum Hoare logic of Zhou, Yu and Ying
\cite{zhou2019applied,zhou2023coqq,feng2023coqqlr}; none existed in Isabelle/HOL, whose
verified algorithms were Deutsch--Jozsa, Grover and the QFT (a recent HOL Light development
treats Grover~\cite{sun2026grover}).  Part~VI of the development (session
\isa{Vern\_Paulsen\_QC\_Algorithms}, the last block of Table~\ref{tab:theories}) builds HHL
on the library of Parts I--V; it goes beyond the notes and is not counted in the audit.

\paragraph{Functional calculus.}  For Hermitian $A$ and $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$ we define
$g(A)$ as the matrix with $g(A)v=g(\lambda)v$ for every eigenvector $v$ with eigenvalue
$\lambda$.  The eigenvectors span, so this determines $g(A)$; no interpolation or
independence-of-decomposition argument is needed.  The spectral theorem of Part~I gives
existence and $g(A)=U\,\mathrm{diag}(g(D))\,U^\dagger$ for every decomposition.
Multiplicativity, $g(A)^\dagger=\bar g(A)$, unitarity for $|g|=1$ and positivity for $g\ge0$
on the spectrum follow by checking eigenvectors.  Instances are $e^{itA}$, with its group
law, and $A^{-1}$ (the pseudo-inverse when $0$ is an eigenvalue, since $1/0=0$ in HOL).

\paragraph{Phase estimation.}  The Fourier matrix $F_N$, $(F_N)_{jk}=\omega^{jk}/\sqrt N$, is
unitary by the roots-of-unity sum.  Controlled powers $\sum_k\ketbra kk\otimes U^k$ act by
phase kickback, and for $Uu=e^{2\pi i\varphi}u$ the circuit leaves the register in
$\sum_j\alpha_j\ket j$ with $\alpha_j=\frac1N\sum_ke^{2\pi ik(\varphi-j/N)}$.  If
$\varphi=j_0/N$ the outcome is $j_0$.  Otherwise the outcome nearest $N\varphi$ (mod $N$) has
probability at least $4/\pi^2$, by Jordan's inequality and $|e^{ix}-1|=2|\sin(x/2)|$.  The
Archive's QFT entry~\cite{manrique2025qft} gives its circuit's output in product form; by
induction over the Kronecker list we show that the circuit \isa{ordered\_QFT}~$n$ equals
$F_{2^n}$, so these theorems hold for the Archive's circuit.

\paragraph{HHL.}  The state space is register $\otimes$ (system $\otimes$ ancilla), so phase
estimation applies unchanged to $e^{it_0A}\otimes I$.  The circuit is $W^\dagger\,R\,W$ with $W$
phase estimation and $R=\sum_k\ketbra kk\otimes I\otimes R(C/\lambda_k)$; register value $k$
stands for $\lambda_k=2\pi k/(t_0N)$, or $2\pi(k-N)/(t_0N)$ for $k\ge N/2$.  Assuming every
eigenvalue of an invertible Hermitian $A$ is some $\lambda_k$, we prove that the output on
$\ket{u}\otimes\ket b\otimes\ket0$ ($u$ the uniform register state) is
\[u\otimes\bigl(\sqrt{I-C^2A^{-2}}\,b\otimes\ket0+C\,A^{-1}b\otimes\ket1\bigr).\]
The proof treats an eigenvector and extends to all $b$ by the fact that two matrices
agreeing on all eigenvectors are equal, so no sums over an eigenbasis appear.  Hence
$x=A^{-1}b$ solves $Ax=b$, the ancilla outcome $\ket1$ has probability $C^2\norm x^2$, and
for $C\le|\lambda|\le\Lambda$ on the spectrum this is at least $(C/\Lambda)^2\norm b^2$,
i.e.\ $1/\kappa^2$ for unit $b$ and $C=\lambda_{\min}$.  A non-Hermitian invertible $A$ is
handled through $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A\\A^\dagger&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, whose
inverse maps $b\oplus0$ to $0\oplus x$.

\paragraph{Inexact phase estimation.}  When $\lambda$ is not a register value, phase
estimation leaves the register in $\sum_j\alpha_j\ket j$, the rotation acts with
$C/\lambda_j$ on the $j$-th branch and uncomputation is inexact.  Writing states as
register sums $\sum_j\ket j\otimes f_j$ (block operators act branch by branch, branches
are orthogonal) gives the exact identity
$\norm{\text{output}-u_N\otimes u\otimes R(C/\lambda)\ket0}^2
 =\norm u^2\sum_j|\alpha_j|^2\norm{R(c_j)\ket0-R(c)\ket0}^2$.
For register values below $C$ the rotation $R(C/\lambda_j)$ is not unitary and the branch
error is unbounded, so the physical circuit clamps the angle to $[-1,1]$; for it, with
$\sum_j|\alpha_j|^2=1$, the tail bound $|\alpha_j|\le1/(2N|\delta_j|)$, Jordan's inequality
and a Lipschitz estimate for $c\mapsto R(c)\ket0$ on $[0,\theta]$, we prove: if
$\lambda\ge2C/\theta$ and $t_0\lambda\le\pi/2$ then the squared distance is at most
$\norm u^2\,\frac{\pi^2}{Nt_0^2}\bigl(\frac{\theta^4}{C^2(1-\theta^2)}+\frac{16}{\lambda^2}\bigr)$
(\isa{hhl\_clamped\_error\_bound}).  The error vectors of distinct eigenvectors are
orthogonal (each lies in $\C^N\otimes\mathrm{span}(u_j)\otimes\C^2$), so with $U$ the
eigenbasis, $U^*E^*EU$ is diagonal for the error operator $E$ and $\varepsilon^2I-E^*E$ is
positive: for every $b$, with the spectrum in $[\lambda_{\min},\pi/2t_0]$ and
$\lambda_{\min}\ge2C/\theta$,
$\norm{\text{output}-\text{exact output}}^2\le
 \frac{\pi^2}{Nt_0^2}\bigl(\frac{\theta^4}{C^2(1-\theta^2)}+\frac{16}{\lambda_{\min}^2}\bigr)\norm b^2$
(\isa{hhl\_clamped\_bound}), with no loss in the dimension.

\paragraph{Sharper constants.}  The bound above is $O(1/N)$ in the squared error, so precision
$\varepsilon$ needs a register of size $N\sim1/\varepsilon^2$.  The uniform register state is the
culprit: its amplitudes decay only like $1/(N\delta)$.  Preparing the register in the sine window
$\psi_\tau=\sqrt{2/N}\,\sin(\pi(\tau+\frac12)/N)$, as in the HHL paper, the window sum has a
closed form and $|\alpha_j|^2\le\pi^2/(2N^4\delta_j^4)$ for $1/N\le|\delta_j|\le\frac12$
(\isa{win\_amp\_tail}).  On good branches the distance is $O(\delta_j^2)$, so each term is at
most $K\,w(N\varphi-j)/N^2$ for a weight $w$ with $\sum_jw(N\varphi-j)\le4+2\pi^2$; clamped and
negative branches have $|\delta_j|\ge\varphi/2$ and contribute $O(1/N^3)$ in total.  For every $b$,
$\norm{\text{output}-\text{exact output}}^2\le
 \bigl(\frac{8\pi^2(2+\pi^2)\theta^4}{N^2t_0^2C^2(1-\theta^2)}+\frac{512\pi^6}{N^3t_0^4\lambda_{\min}^4}\bigr)\norm b^2$
(\isa{hhl\_window\_bound}), so $N\ge Q/\varepsilon$ gives error at most $\varepsilon\norm b$
(\isa{hhl\_window\_precision}).

\paragraph{The surrounding algorithm.}  The circuit uses $e^{iAt_0}$ only through controlled
powers, so we write it in a small language of fixed gates and controlled oracle calls with a
semantics and a call count: HHL makes $2(N-1)$ calls, and replacing the oracle by any unitary $V$
with $\norm{Vv-e^{iAt_0}v}\le\eta\norm v$ moves the output by at most $2(N-1)\eta$
(\isa{hhl\_sim\_robust}, from a general lemma: each call adds at most $\eta$).  End to end the
output is within $(\varepsilon+2(N-1)\eta)\norm b$ of the exact one.  The answer is read by
post-selecting the ancilla; normalisation is stable, so the post-selected state is within
$2\delta/(C\norm{A^{-1}b})$ of $\psi\otimes A^{-1}b/\norm{A^{-1}b}\otimes\ket1$
(\isa{hhl\_readout}).  Amplitude amplification is proved in the plane of the good and bad
components ($k$ rounds give $\sin^2((2k+1)\theta)$), and as a circuit it uses the HHL circuit and
its inverse once per round.  Theorem \isa{hhl\_complete} combines state preparation, the window,
the simulation oracle, amplification and readout: success probability at least $1-p$ after
$k\le\pi^2/(4Ct_0)$ rounds, i.e.\ $O(\kappa)$, with $(2k+1)\cdot2(N-1)$ oracle calls.

\paragraph{The paper's error analysis.}  Appendix~A of the HHL paper uses a three-level flag
register (nothing, well, ill) set by filter functions $f,g$.  We formalise $f,g$ and
$\ket{h(\lambda)}$, Lemma~2 ($\lambda\mapsto\ket{h(\lambda)}$ is $\frac\pi2\kappa$-Lipschitz; the constant is
attained) and Lemma~3, the relative bound
$|f-\tilde f|^2+|g-\tilde g|^2\le c\,\kappa^2(\lambda-\tilde\lambda)^2(f^2+g^2)$.  The paper states $c=\pi^2/2$;
this is false.  On $[1/2\kappa,1/\kappa)$ the proof uses $f^2+g^2=\frac12$, but there $f^2+g^2=\frac14$, and the
ratio of the two sides tends to $\pi^2$.  We prove the lemma with $c=\pi^2$, which is sharp, by gluing
region estimates (\isa{lemma3}).  With the window register and the wrapped distances of the
register eigenvalues, all three parts of Theorem~1 follow for every $b$ with explicit constants:
$\norm{(\tilde U-U)b}\le3\pi^2\sqrt{4+2\pi^2}\,\kappa\norm b/t$, and the post-selected states are within
$12\pi^2\sqrt{4+2\pi^2}\,\kappa/t$ of the ideal ones, $t=Nt_0$ the total evolution time
(\isa{flag\_bound}, \isa{flag\_post\_normalized}, \isa{flag\_well\_normalized}).  The audit of the
paper's results against the formalisation is part of the notes audit.  The concentration bound
(A32), $|\alpha_{k|j}|\le8\pi/\delta^2$, holds for $2\pi\le|\delta|\le N$ (\isa{win\_amp\_A32}).  For the
paper's run-time claim we work in its normalisation (spectrum in $[1/\kappa,1]$): total evolution time
$t\ge c\kappa/\varepsilon$ gives accuracy $\varepsilon$ for the post-selected state, the success amplitude is at
least $1/(4\kappa)$, and amplification takes at most $\pi\kappa$ rounds, so the oracle is run for total time
$O(\kappa^2/\varepsilon)$ and the state preparation $O(\kappa)$ times (\isa{hhl\_paper\_runtime}).  Running
amplification without knowing $p$ rests on BHMT's averaging lemma (\isa{bhmt\_quarter}): stages with a
random number of rounds below $1,2,4,\dots$, stopped at the first success, succeed with probability at
least $1/4$ in fewer than $8\sqrt2\,\kappa$ rounds (\isa{doubling\_schedule\_kappa}).  The
reduction from circuits to matrix inversion behind the optimality results is formalised as a
construction (\isa{red\_inverse}, \isa{red\_A\_bounds}, \isa{red\_clock\_prob}, \isa{red\_post\_state}),
with two corrections: the undo gates are $U^*_{T+1-t}$, not $U^*_{3T+1-t}$, and the condition number is
$\coth\frac1{2T}\le2T+1$ rather than at most $2T$.

\paragraph{Theorems 4--6.}  BQP, PSPACE, BPP and PP are not in the AFP (\isa{Cook\_Levin} has P and
NP), so each optimality theorem is formalised up to the external fact it cites.  Clock in $[T,2T)$
and outcome $P$ has probability $e^{-2}/(1+e^{-2}+e^{-4})\norm{PU_T\cdots U_1\psi}^2$
(\isa{red\_M0\_prob}); the Hermitian dilation (A34) keeps the bounds $1\pm e^{-1/T}$
(\isa{red\_dilation}); and a permutation fixing $\ket0$ turns $M_0$ into a first-qubit measurement
(\isa{red\_first\_qubit}).  For Theorem~4 the speedup iteration gives
$T_\ell\le\max(g^{2/\delta},T_0^{(1-\delta/2)^m})$ with $n_i\le n_0+i\log18+\frac2\delta\log T_0$
(\isa{speedup\_iteration}, \isa{qubit\_growth}), hence a $\mathrm{poly}(n)$-gate simulation of every
computation with $T\le2^{2n}$ (\isa{speedup\_collapse}).  The relativised part ends with polynomially
many queries for search on $2^n$ inputs, refuted with the AFP bound $sN\le(5q+11)^2$ of
\emph{Compressed Oracles}, imported in a separate session (\isa{search\_speedup\_impossible},
\isa{search\_query\_bound}).  Theorems~5 and~6(2) reduce to growth-rate arguments with the cited
lower bounds as hypotheses ($3\alpha+4\beta\ge\frac12$, $\alpha+\beta\ge1$; \isa{simon\_exponents},
\isa{parity\_exponents}), and Theorem~6(1) to the counting state, whose last qubit reads $1$ with
probability $\#\{z:\varphi(z)\}/2^n$ (\isa{count\_state\_prob}).  Not formalised: TQBF's
PSPACE-completeness, $\mathrm{BQP}\subseteq\mathrm{PSPACE}$, and the classical Simon and parity bounds.
Four errata: the proof's $m=\frac2\delta\log(2n)/\log\log n$ rounds do not give $T_m\le n_0$ (for $n_0=2^{64}$,
$\delta=\frac12$ the bound is still above $2^{64}$; $m\ge\frac2\delta\ln(2n_0)$ works); (A34) cannot satisfy the
demanded $\kappa^{-1}I\le A$, its diagonal being zero; in (A37) the top half of $\vec y$ is always $0$,
so its first qubit carries no information; and Theorem~6(2)'s $\epsilon=2^{-n-2}$ cannot separate counts
$C$ and $C+1$ once the clock factor $\le\frac14$ is included (\isa{paper\_rounds\_insufficient},
\isa{herm\_embed\_diag}, \isa{paper\_precision\_insufficient}).

\paragraph{A verified program.}  The results above are statements about matrices.  To show that
a program computes them we use the quantum while-language and Hoare logic of the AFP entry
\emph{QHLProver}, which is sound and complete for partial correctness and works on the same
matrices.  The program \isa{hhl\_program} initialises all registers (QHLProver has no
initialisation statement; we build one from a computational-basis measurement and swaps,
\isa{hoare\_init}), applies eight gates (prepare $\ket b=U_b\ket0$, the Hadamard layer, the
controlled evolution, $F^\dagger$, the controlled rotation, $F$, the inverse controlled evolution,
the Hadamard layer) and measures the ancilla.  The state after each gate is computed for every
$b$; phase estimation leaves $\sum_j\ket j\otimes\Pi_jb$ with $\Pi_j$ the spectral projector at the
grid eigenvalue (\isa{hhl\_steps}, \isa{hhl\_step\_triples}).  With the proof rules we derive
$\{C^2\norm{A^{-1}b}^2\,I\}\ \mathit{hhl\_program}\ \{P_1\}$ (success probability) and
$\{I\}\ \mathit{hhl\_program}\ \{P_0+\ket t\bra t\}$ with $t=\ket0\otimes A^{-1}b/\norm{A^{-1}b}\otimes\ket1$
(on success the state is exactly the solution); since loop-free programs preserve the trace,
both hold for total correctness (\isa{hhl\_success\_total}, \isa{hhl\_correct\_total}).  The
repeat-until-success program, a \textbf{while} loop around the circuit, satisfies
$\{I\}\ \mathit{hhl\_rus}\ \{\ket t\bra t\}$ (\isa{hhl\_rus\_correct}), and, since its body re-initialises the
registers, every round is an independent trial with success probability $p=C^2\norm{A^{-1}b}^2$: the loop
terminates with probability one, so the triple holds for total correctness when $C\neq0$
(\isa{hhl\_rus\_total}; QHLProver has no total-correctness rule for loops, so this is derived from
the semantics of a loop whose body resets the state, \isa{while\_reset\_total}), it exits after
exactly $m$ failed rounds with probability $(1-p)^mp$ in the target state
(\isa{hhl\_rus\_prog\_rounds}), and the expected number of rounds is $1/p$
(\isa{hhl\_rus\_expected\_rounds}).  The register is prepared by
$H^{\otimes n}$, which takes $\ket{0\cdots0}$ to the uniform state (\isa{hadamard\_layer\_zero}, from
\emph{Isabelle Marries Dirac}).  The evolution $e^{iAt_0}$ enters as a gate, as in the rest of
Part~VI.  The theorems hold for every gate list with the circuit matrix of the eight gates, and
\isa{HHL\_Gates} gives one in elementary gates: $H^{\otimes k}$ as $k$ single-qubit Hadamards, the
controlled evolution $\sum_c\ket c\bra c\otimes U^c$ as one controlled $U^{2^j}$ per clock qubit
(\isa{ctrl\_pow\_qubits}; $2^k-1$ controlled-$U$ calls, \isa{ctrl\_pow\_queries}), and $F$ as the
AFP's gate-level Fourier circuit, flattened into Hadamards, controlled phase rotations and swaps
(\isa{qft\_circuit}); the whole circuit has $(2k^3+16k+6)/3$ gates (\isa{length\_hhl\_elem\_gates}), the
cubic term being the Fourier circuit, whose controlled rotations are routed through nearest-neighbour
swaps.  Apart from the controlled powers, the preparation of $\ket b$ and the
controlled rotation, every gate is a one- or two-qubit unitary on adjacent wires
(\isa{hhl\_elem\_gate\_kinds}), and success probability, correctness and the loop carry over
(\isa{hhl\_elem\_correct\_total}).  \isa{HHL\_Preconditioned} runs the program on a preconditioned
system $BAx=Bb$: $BA$ need not be Hermitian, so the program takes its Hermitian dilation and the
right-hand side $Bb/\norm{Bb}\oplus0$; on success the system register holds $0\oplus x/\norm x$
for the solution of the original $Ax=b$ (\isa{hhl\_elem\_precond\_correct}), with success
probability $C^2\norm x^2/\norm{Bb}^2\ge(C/s)^2$ when $\norm{BAv}\le s\norm v$
(\isa{hhl\_elem\_precond\_bound}).  The programs so far assume that every eigenvalue is a register
value; \isa{HHL\_Paper\_Program} removes this.  The circuits of the paper's regime (window register,
eigenvalues anywhere in the spectral window, an $\eta$-accurate evolution oracle, the filtered
three-level version) become gate lists of the same semantics (\isa{qgates\_sem}) and run as
programs whose postcondition $\ket t\bra t$ measures fidelity with the target: one run gives
$\{(Ct_0/\pi)^2(1-e^2)\,I\}$, where $e$ is the error bound of \isa{hhl\_complete}, and the
repeat-until-success loop gives $\{(1-e^2)\,I\}$ with termination (\isa{hhl\_apx\_rus}); the
filtered version gives $\{(1-\varepsilon^2)\,I\}$ in the run-time normalisation (\isa{hhl\_flag\_rus}),
and amplitude amplification is a program with at most $\pi\kappa$ rounds (\isa{hhl\_flag\_amplified}).
\isa{HHL\_Gate\_Refinements} splits the controlled rotation into one ancilla rotation per clock
value (\isa{hhl\_split\_gate\_kinds}), prepares the window state with one Householder reflection
(\isa{hhl\_apx\_program\_prepared}) and replaces the Fourier gates of the amplified circuit by
the gate-level circuit (\isa{hhl\_apx\_fine}).  \isa{HHL\_Uniform\_Rotation} finally writes the rotation stage
$\sum_c\ket c\bra c\otimes I\otimes R(\theta_c)$ itself in elementary gates: with Walsh--Hadamard
coefficients it is one ancilla rotation and $2^k-1$ parity-controlled rotations, each parity being
computed into the last clock qubit by nearest-neighbour CNOT and SWAP gates (\isa{ucr\_circuit}).  In
the resulting program every gate is elementary, an oracle call, the preparation of $\ket b$, or a
rotation of the ancilla controlled by the last clock qubit (\isa{hhl\_fine\_gate\_kinds},
\isa{hhl\_apx\_fine2}).

\paragraph{Rectangular systems and the Discussion.}  For $A\in\mathbb C^{M\times N}$ the algorithm runs on
$H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A\\A^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and inverts it on its non-zero
eigenspaces.  The pseudo-inverse maps $b\oplus0$ to $0\oplus x$ with $A^*Ax=A^*b$ and $x$ in the
range of $A^*$, so $x$ is the minimum-norm solution when $Ax=b$ is solvable and a least-squares
solution otherwise (\isa{pinv\_solution}, \isa{normal\_eq\_least\_squares}); the proof uses
$Jg(H)J=g(-H)$ for the parity $J$.  For Section~IV we prove that rotation angle $h(\lambda)$ yields
$h(A)\ket b$, that expectations, sampled distributions and $k$-fold copies move by $O(\norm{\tilde x-x})$,
and that the SWAP test, built from $H\otimes I$ and a controlled SWAP, reads $\ket0$ with probability
$(1+|\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle|^2)/2$.  The estimation procedure of Appendix~A5 is formalised with an
expectation operator over $n$ independent bits: mean, variance and Chebyshev give $3/\varepsilon^2$
samples (\isa{estimate\_expectation}).  Two more errata: the paper's $1/(3\varepsilon^2)$ samples are too few
(for $\varepsilon=\frac14$, $p=\frac12$ and six samples the estimate fails with probability $\frac{11}{16}$), and
its preconditioned system $(AB)c=Bb$ is neither left nor right preconditioning; in a $2\times2$
example its unique solution gives no solution of $Ax=b$ (\isa{paper\_precondition\_wrong}).

\paragraph{What remains.}  The window state and $\ket b$ are prepared by given unitaries.
Hamiltonian simulation enters as an $\eta$-accurate oracle: building one from sparse access
(Trotter-type formulas) and counting its gates is outside the matrix framework, so the
complexity statement is in oracle calls.

\section{Conclusion}

Taking a complete set of lecture notes as the specification turned out to be a productive
way to build a library: it fixes the scope, orders the work, and makes completeness
measurable.  The cost is visible in the audit: a course mentions many results it does
not prove, and a formalisation faithful to the notes must decide, item by item, what to
prove, what to restate, and what to leave.  The commentary pass that produced the
generated document is the record of those decisions; we recommend it as a deliverable for
any formalisation of a text, because it is the only place where the informal and the
formal statements are read side by side.
