\begin{abstract}
We report on a formalisation in Isabelle/HOL of the mathematical content of Vern
Paulsen's 2011 lecture course \emph{Quantum Computing and Completely Positive Maps}
(34 lectures): finite-dimensional Hilbert space theory, the postulates of quantum
mechanics for pure and mixed states, partial traces, completely positive maps with
Choi's theorem and the Choi--Kraus representation, the extreme points of the sets of
unital and trace-preserving completely positive maps (Choi's Theorem~3), the
Hahn--Banach theorem on matrix spaces, and the separating-hyperplane theory of
entanglement witnesses.  The development comprises twenty-eight theories and about a
thousand checked statements on top of the Jordan-Normal-Form and \IMD libraries; it
builds without \texttt{sorry} or axioms and passes an oracle audit.  A lecture-by-lecture
audit records, for each statement in the notes, its formal counterpart or the reason
it is absent, and an Isabelle-generated document reproduces the notes with the formal
statements in place of the informal proofs.  We then ask what this body of verified
mathematics contributes towards a machine-checked proof of the Harrow--Hassidim--Lloyd
(HHL) algorithm in Isabelle: the spectral theorem, the measurement calculus, tensor
products and partial traces are in place and in a representation compatible with the
existing Isabelle quantum libraries, while Hamiltonian simulation, phase estimation,
the controlled eigenvalue inversion and the error analysis remain to be done.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Machine-checked quantum computing in Isabelle/HOL has so far concentrated on
algorithms: \IMD~\cite{bordg2021imd,bordg2020afp} formalises qubits, gates, the
no-cloning theorem, teleportation and the Deutsch and Deutsch--Jozsa algorithms on
concrete matrices; the quantum Hoare logic of Liu et al.~\cite{liu2019qhl,liu2019afp}
verifies Grover's algorithm; Manrique~\cite{manrique2025qft} adds the quantum Fourier
transform.  The mathematics that a graduate course on the subject spends most of its
time on---positive matrices, the density-matrix formulation, partial traces,
completely positive maps and their Choi matrices, the convex geometry of channels and
of separable states---is covered unevenly.  Unruh's libraries~\cite{caballero2021cbo,
unruh2024tensor,unruh2025kraus} develop much of this for arbitrary Hilbert spaces, in a
type-based representation of operators; Echenim's entry~\cite{echenim2021pm}
formalises projective measurements.

This paper describes a different kind of project: the end-to-end formalisation of one
set of lecture notes, Paulsen's \emph{Lectures on Quantum Computing and Completely
Positive Maps} (Houston, Fall 2011)~\cite{paulsen2011notes}, taken as the specification.
The notes are an unedited transcription of 34 lectures in five parts (basic
mathematics; introduction to quantum computing; the theory of completely positive maps;
entanglement; error correction).  Following them lecture by lecture forces one to
formalise the whole supporting theory rather than the shortest path to a headline
theorem, and it produces a natural, externally fixed measure of completeness: the audit
of Section~\ref{sec:audit}.

The project started as an autoformalisation experiment and was finished by the author
with AI assistance; everything reported here is checked by Isabelle2025-2 with the
strict build option \texttt{quick\_and\_dirty = false}, and the principal theorems have
been checked to depend on no oracles.  The sources, the audit, and the generated
document are available in the project repository.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/brydustin/Quantum_Computing}}

Contributions.  (1)~A verified formalisation of Parts I--IV of the notes and of the
elementary part of Part~V: in particular Schur's theorem, the spectral theorem for
Hermitian matrices, four characterisations of positivity, Cholesky's reduction, the
measurement postulates and POVMs, the characterisation of density matrices by
ensembles, partial traces and their compatibility with local measurements, Choi's
theorem and the Choi--Kraus representation of channels, the adjoint duality between
unital and trace-preserving maps, Choi's Theorem~3 on extreme points, the Hahn--Banach
theorem for matrix spaces, and the entanglement-witness characterisation of
separability.  (2)~A lecture-by-lecture audit that classifies every definition and
result of the notes as formalised, formalised in a variant form, not formalised, or
deliberately omitted.  (3)~A document, generated by Isabelle from the theories, that
restates the notes with the formal statements replacing the informal proofs.
(4)~An assessment of how far this material goes towards a verified HHL algorithm.

\section{The notes and the formalisation method}

\paragraph{Representation.}  All objects of the notes are concrete: vectors in $\C^n$ and
matrices in $M_{m,n}$.  We use the Jordan-Normal-Form library~\cite{thiemann2015jnf}:
vectors are \isa{complex Matrix.vec} with a dimension hypothesis
\isa{dim\_vec v = n}, matrices are \isa{complex Matrix.mat} with carrier hypotheses
\isa{A $\in$ carrier\_mat m n}.  Dimensions are hypotheses rather than types, which is
how the notes treat them and which is the convention of \IMD, so that its gates,
inner product, adjoint (\isa{dagger}) and tensor products can be reused directly.  Maps
$\Phi:M_n\to M_d$ are HOL functions \isa{complex Matrix.mat $\Rightarrow$ complex
Matrix.mat} constrained only on the carrier; linearity, positivity and complete
positivity are predicates on such functions.

Two places in the notes genuinely need topology (compactness and the separation of a
point from a closed convex cone, Days 22 and 27--28).  For these we introduce a typed
wrapper \isa{('a,'m,'n) lmat} of matrices of fixed dimension, instantiate the
HOL-Analysis type classes up to \isa{euclidean\_space} and \isa{heine\_borel}, and
transport the relevant statements across the wrapper.  The carrier-predicate world and
the typed world coexist; the former carries almost all of the development, the latter
is used only where HOL-Analysis is applied.

\paragraph{Fidelity.}  Statements are formalised as close to the notes as the
representation permits.  The audit distinguishes faithful statements from
\emph{variants}; the main variants are: the predicate \isa{psd} carries Hermiticity as an
explicit conjunct (equivalent by the notes' own Theorem that $\braket{x}{Hx}\in\R$ for
all $x$ iff $H=H^*$); Cholesky's reduction adds the hypothesis $p_{11}>0$ that the notes
leave implicit; linear maps are given with explicit additivity and homogeneity
hypotheses on the carrier; and Choi's Theorem~3 is proved in its trace-preserving form
with a fixed Kraus list of linearly independent operators, see Section~\ref{sec:choi3}.
Where the notes themselves are inconsistent we formalise the correct statement and say
so in the commentary (the notes claim $H^2=X$ on Day~10 and correct it to $H^2=I$ on
Day~11; they use two sign conventions for the Pauli matrix $Y$).

\paragraph{Verification discipline.}  The project builds as three Isabelle sessions
with \texttt{quick\_and\_dirty = false}, so no \texttt{sorry} can survive.  We also
ran an oracle audit (\isa{Thm\_Deps.all\_oracles} over the principal theorems).  This
turned up a methodological point worth recording: under \texttt{quick\_and\_dirty =
true}, Isabelle's \isa{Goal.prove\_sorry} discharges package-internal goals of
\isa{fun} and \isa{record} definitions with \isa{Skip\_Proof.cheat\_tac}, so every
theorem downstream of such a definition reports the oracle \isa{Pure.skip\_proof} even
in a development that contains no \texttt{sorry} at all.  Oracle audits are only
meaningful on builds with the strict option.

\paragraph{Lectures 1--10: linear algebra and the postulates.}  Besides the inner-product
algebra, matrix units and the trace (\isa{Tr\_cyclic}, \isa{hs\_inner\_prod\_as\_trace}),
the first part proves the projection formula $P=\sum\ketbra{v_i}{v_i}$ and Parseval
(\isa{parseval\_norm}), the characterisations of unitaries, Householder unitaries and
Schur's lemma (\isa{schur\_lemma}), Schur's triangularisation (\isa{schur\_theorem}) and
$\Tr A=\sum\lambda_i$, the spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices
(\isa{hermitian\_spectral\_decomp}: $U^\dagger HU$ is a real diagonal matrix), and the
positivity theory: $P\ge0$ iff Hermitian with non-negative eigenvalues
(\isa{psd\_iff\_nonneg\_eigenvalues}), iff $P=\sum\ketbra{v_i}{v_i}$
(\isa{psd\_kraus\_decomp}, \isa{outer\_sum\_psd}), positive definiteness iff the $v_i$ span
(\isa{pd\_iff\_spanning}), the positive square root, and Cholesky's reduction
(\isa{cholesky\_reduction}).  Tensor products are Kronecker products with bilinearity,
the multiplicativity of inner products and the orthonormal product basis
(\isa{tensor\_onb}).  The postulates are definitions: \isa{quantum\_state},
\isa{evolve}, \isa{measurement\_system}, \isa{meas\_prob}, with the probabilities summing
to one, POVMs with their square-root measurement systems (\isa{povm\_from\_sqrt}),
perfect distinguishability of orthogonal states, and local measurements in composite
systems (\isa{composite\_measurement\_prob}).

\paragraph{Lectures 11--22: channels and Choi's theorem.}  The no-cloning theorem
(\isa{no\_cloning}) and the existence of oracles $U_f$ (\isa{oracle\_exists}) open the
second part.  Ensembles and their density matrices are defined, and density matrices are
characterised as the positive matrices of trace one (\isa{density\_matrix\_iff\_ensemble},
via the spectral ensemble); the ensembles of a pure state consist of multiples of the
state (\isa{pure\_ensemble\_parallel}).  Partial traces are defined by the block formulas
of the notes; they are trace preserving and positivity preserving
(\isa{partial\_trace\_B\_is\_density}) and compatible with local measurements:
$\Tr((M^\dagger M\otimes I)\rho)=\Tr(M^\dagger M\,\mathrm{tr}_B\rho)$
(\isa{partial\_trace\_measurement}).  The coordinate form of
$L(\mathcal H_A)\otimes L(\mathcal H_B)\cong L(\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B)$ is an
explicit linear isomorphism.  Measurement maps $\rho\mapsto\sum M_\alpha\rho M_\alpha^\dagger$
preserve density matrices; Kraus maps are completely positive (\isa{kraus\_is\_cp}), with
complete positivity defined through the block amplification $\Phi^{(p)}$.  Choi's theorem
is \isa{choi\_theorem}: a linear $\Phi$ on the carrier is completely positive iff its Choi
matrix $(\Phi(E_{ij}))_{ij}$ is positive, and \isa{kraus\_rep\_of\_psd\_choi} extracts a
Kraus representation with $nd$ operators from a positive Choi matrix, which gives the
Choi--Kraus characterisation of channels (\isa{choi\_kraus\_cptp}) and the Kraus-rank bound
(\isa{kraus\_rank\_bound}).  The Hilbert--Schmidt adjoint of a Kraus map is the Kraus map
of the adjoint operators (\isa{kraus\_adjoint\_correct}), and $\Phi$ is unital iff $\Phi^*$ is
trace preserving (\isa{cp\_unital\_iff\_adjoint\_tp}); positive functionals are
completely positive (\isa{positive\_scalar\_is\_cp}); the sets UCP and CPTP are convex.

\paragraph{Lecture 23: Choi's Theorem 3.}\label{sec:choi3}  Choi's characterisation of the
extreme points of UCP~\cite{choi1975} is formalised in its trace-preserving form, for a
channel given by a Kraus list $V_1,\dots,V_r$ of linearly independent operators
(\isa{choi\_theorem\_3\_cptp\_iff}): $\Phi$ is extreme if and only if $\{V_j^\dagger V_i\}$ is
linearly independent over $\C$.  (The perturbation argument of the notes produces a
real-coefficient relation; a complex relation is first turned into a Hermitian one, using
that the family $\{V_j^\dagger V_i\}$ is closed under adjoints, so both directions use the
same notion of independence.)  Two aspects deserve comment.  First, because HOL functions are only
constrained on the carrier, the naive definition of an extreme point (``$\Phi$ is not the
midpoint of two \emph{different} CPTP maps'') is vacuous: any CPTP map agrees on the
carrier with maps that differ from it elsewhere.  Extremality is therefore defined through
disagreement on the carrier (\isa{extreme\_CPTP\_carrier}).  Second, the notes' proof uses
the unitary freedom of Kraus representations (their ``Choi 2''), which was formalised only
afterwards (\isa{choi\_2\_unitary\_freedom} in \isa{Day21\_Unitary\_Freedom}).  The forward
direction instead goes through a CP Radon--Nikodym theorem
(\isa{cp\_radon\_nikodym\_linear}): if $\Psi$ and $\Phi-\Psi$ are completely positive and
$\Phi(X)=\sum_iV_iXV_i^\dagger$, then $\Psi(X)=\sum_{ij}T_{ij}V_iXV_j^\dagger$ for a positive
matrix $T$.  Its proof reduces to a statement about positive matrices dominated by an
outer sum, $0\le B\le VV^\dagger$ implies $B=VTV^\dagger$ with $T\ge0$, which we prove with
the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse of $VV^\dagger$ built from the spectral theorem: the
identities $AA^+A=A$ and, for $0\le B\le A$, $AA^+B=B$ follow from the kernel inclusion
$\ker A\subseteq\ker B$, and $T=(A^+V)^\dagger B(A^+V)$.

\paragraph{Lectures 25--28: Hahn--Banach and entanglement witnesses.}  The notes state the
Hahn--Banach theorem for bounded functionals on normed spaces and use it in Arveson's
extension theorem.  We prove it for matrix spaces with the Hilbert--Schmidt norm
(\isa{matrix\_hahn\_banach\_lmat}): HOL's Hahn--Banach theorem gives a norm-preserving
real-linear extension of the real part, and the Bohnenblust--Sobczyk formula
$f(x)=F(x)-iF(ix)$ recovers a complex-linear extension with the same bound.  Arveson's
correspondence and extension theorem are not formalised.  The entanglement theory of
Days~27--28 is: the separable matrices form a closed convex cone
(\isa{separable\_cone\_lmat\_closed}, using the closedness of the positive matrices, the
compactness of the product density matrices and a Carath\'eodory bound on the number of
generators), so a non-separable $\rho$ is separated from the cone by a real functional,
which becomes a Hermitian witness $W$ with $\Re\Tr(W\rho)<0\le\Re\Tr(W\sigma)$ for all
separable $\sigma$ (\isa{entanglement\_witness\_hermitian'}); this yields the
Horodecki-type characterisation~\cite{horodecki1996}: a Hermitian $\rho$ is separable
iff every such witness is non-negative on it (\isa{separable\_iff\_witness}), with a
strict version for density matrices.  The notes phrase the criterion through positive
maps, which needs the Arveson correspondence; the witness form is the equivalent
statement that does not.

\paragraph{Lectures 29--32: error correction.}  Hamming weight and distance, the parity
check and repetition codes, the Pauli matrices and their orthogonality, the bit-flip,
phase-flip and bit-phase-flip channels (as UCP and CPTP maps), the pure-state fidelity,
and the code words of the three-qubit codes are formalised.  The recovery theorems for
the three-qubit code and the Shor code, and the Knill--Laflamme conditions of the last
lectures, are not.

\section{The audit}\label{sec:audit}

The file \texttt{NOTES\_AUDIT.md} in the repository lists, for each of the 34 lectures,
every definition, theorem, proposition, corollary, example and remark of the notes with
its formal counterpart.  Each item is classified: $\checkmark$ faithful, $\sim$ formalised
in a variant form, $\times$ not formalised, or $-$ deliberately not formalised
(overviews, history, numerical examples, classical asides such as stochastic matrices
or cyclic codes).  Of the 170 items, 11 are deliberate omissions; of the remaining 159
mathematical items, 122 are faithful, 21 are variants and 16 are gaps.\footnote{Counts as
of the second close-out of October 2026; the audit file is the authoritative record.}  The audit
drove a final round of proofs: small statements omitted along the way (the converses
isometry~$\Rightarrow$~unitary, positive definiteness iff positive eigenvalues, the
expected value $\braket{\psi}{H\psi}$ of an observable, the phase invariance of
$\ketbra\psi\psi$, that $\rho_1\otimes\rho_2$ is a density matrix, the unambiguous
discrimination of linearly independent states, and the complex-coefficient form of
Choi's Theorem~3) were proved during the close-out and are counted as faithful above.
A second pass closed the medium-sized gaps that the notes prove in a page, each in its
own theory (the lower block of Table~\ref{tab:theories}): a linear map sends density
matrices to density matrices iff it is positive and trace preserving
(\isa{density\_preserving\_iff\_positive\_tp}); the transpose on $M_2$ is positive but not
$2$-positive, with the notes' witness $(E_{ij})$; the Hadamard power
$H^{\otimes N}\ket{0\cdots0}=2^{-N/2}\sum_J\ket J$ and the counting probability
$p_0=(M/2^N)^2$ of Day~12, for any unitary acting as the oracle on the basis; the Pauli
expansion identity $\sum|a_k|^2=1$ for unitaries; the bound $\crk(\Phi)\le n$ for extreme
CPTP maps (linear independence bounds the length, through the vector-space library of
the Jordan-Normal-Form entry); the two-qubit Horodecki--Peres criterion, $(\mathrm{id}\otimes
t)(\ketbra vv)\ge0$ iff $v$ is a product vector, with the witness value
$-2|v_0v_3-v_1v_2|^2$; and the three-qubit bit-flip code, whose syndrome projections,
CPTP recovery map and recovery theorem $R(\ketbra{\psi_i}{\psi_i})=\ketbra\psi\psi$ for at
most one flip are proved on explicit $8\times8$ matrices.  A third pass added the
structural results of lectures 20--22 and 32--34: two rank-one decompositions
$\sum v_jv_j^*=\sum w_lw_l^*$ with the $v_j$ independent are related by an isometry, and
hence two Kraus representations of one map by an isometry (Choi's Theorem~2,
\isa{choi\_2\_unitary\_freedom}, proved through the Choi matrix); a Kraus representation
of minimal length $\crk(\Phi)$ is linearly independent; the Choi rank theorem
$\crk(\Phi)=\rank P_\Phi$ (\isa{choi\_rank\_eq\_rank}, with the rank taken in the
Jordan-Normal-Form vector-space library); the general recovery proposition of Day~32,
$R(\ketbra\varphi\varphi)=(\sum_i|a_i|^2)\ketbra\psi\psi$ for $\varphi=\sum_ia_iU_i\psi$ with
$PU_i^*U_jP=\delta_{ij}P$ (\isa{recovery\_linear\_combination}; the notes assume unitary
$U_i$, but only $PU_i^*U_iP=P$ is used); and the Knill--Laflamme theory: the equivalence
between correcting the states of $V$ and fixing $PXP$, Theorem~1 in both directions
(\isa{knill\_laflamme}; necessity through Choi's Theorem~2, sufficiency by diagonalising
$(\alpha_{ij})$ and applying the recovery proposition to the rescaled mixed errors, which
need not be restrictions of unitaries) and the closure of the corrected errors under
linear combinations.  Finally the Shor code: one CPTP map corrects every single-qubit
unitary error on each of the nine qubits (\isa{shor\_code\_theorem}).  The $2^9$-dimensional
space is never handled entrywise; nine qubits are three blocks of three, each single-qubit
operator acts inside one block, inner products factor over the blocks, and the
orthogonality of the $22$ error images reduces to the distinctness of $44$ block labels,
decided by \isa{code\_simp}.  Here the formalisation found a false statement: the Day~32
proposition that distinct 1-Paulis map the code space to orthogonal subspaces fails for
$Z_1\ne Z_2$, which agree on the code space (\isa{shor\_notes\_Z1\_Z2}); the notes correct it
on Day~33.  The environment model of Day~17 is proved as stated:
$\operatorname{tr}_E(U(\rho\otimes\ketbra\varphi\varphi)U^*)=\sum_iW_i\rho W_i^*$ with
$\sum_iW_i^*W_i=I$ (\isa{environment\_model}).  Last, the operator-system part of the
course: Arveson's theorem that a linear map on an operator system is completely positive iff
it is $d$-positive iff $S_\Phi$ is positive, the correspondence $\Phi\leftrightarrow S_\Phi$,
the extension theorem (\isa{arveson\_extension}) with the Kraus form of completely positive
maps on operator systems, and the positive-map criterion for separability
(\isa{separable\_iff\_positive\_maps}).  The extension is not proved as in the notes (a state
of norm one, Hahn--Banach, and a reduction to the unital case): the positive functional
$S_\Phi$ on $M_d(S)$ is extended directly, by separating the positive definite cone from its
kernel with HOL-Analysis' separating hyperplane theorem in the Euclidean space of typed
matrices, which gives the extension $X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(DX)$ with $D\ge0$.  All items
of the first audit's gap list are now closed; the 16 remaining gaps are examples, a few small
propositions, and the two lemmas that only the notes' route to the extension theorem uses.

Beyond the omissions, the audit documents where the formalisation strengthens the
notes (strict witnesses for density matrices; Cholesky with the missing hypothesis;
the vacuity of the naive extreme-point definition) and where it changes the route of a
proof (Choi's Theorem~3 through CP Radon--Nikodym).

\section{The generated document}

Isabelle's document preparation turns the theories into a PDF in which proofs are
folded and the commentary appears between the formal statements.  During this project
we rewrote the top-level comments of all theories as document markup: each lecture is a
section, and before each group of formal statements a text block restates the notes'
definitions and results in their own words and LaTeX, says which Isabelle statements
correspond to them and how they differ, and names the audit items for what is missing.
The result is a 2011 lecture course in which every displayed theorem has been checked,
organised by theory rather than strictly by date because later lectures reuse earlier
infrastructure; a reading guide at the front gives the map from lectures to theories.
The document is produced by a fourth session, \isa{Vern\_Paulsen\_QC\_Notes}, through
Isabelle's \texttt{document\_theories} mechanism, so it is regenerated and re-checked by
every build.

\section{Relation to the Archive of Formal Proofs}

\IMD~\cite{bordg2020afp} and the quantum Fourier transform entry~\cite{manrique2025qft}
use the same matrix representation as this project (\isa{complex Matrix.mat} with
carriers), and we import both; our tensor-product lemmas (mixed product, adjoint,
unitarity, product bases), measurement calculus and density-matrix theory extend them
in the same style.  The quantum Hoare logic entry~\cite{liu2019afp} contains a positivity
predicate, the Loewner order and a decomposition of positive matrices, overlapping with
our Days~4 and~18, but no Choi theory.  Unruh's \emph{Complex Bounded Operators},
\emph{Tensor Products in Hilbert Spaces} and \emph{Kraus Maps}
\cite{caballero2021cbo,unruh2024tensor,unruh2025kraus} are far more general---arbitrary
Hilbert spaces, trace-class and Hilbert--Schmidt operators, the spectral theorem for
compact operators, Kraus families for possibly infinite-dimensional channels---but live
in a type-based representation of operators (\isa{'a $\Rightarrow_{CL}$ 'b}); they contain
neither Choi's theorem nor the extreme-point and separability results, and connecting
them to the matrix world would require a transfer layer of the kind our \isa{lmat}
wrapper provides in miniature.  Echenim's projective measurements~\cite{echenim2021pm}
are formulated on \isa{complex\^{}'n} vectors.  To our knowledge the results of Days~19--28
(Choi's theorem in its matrix form with explicit Kraus extraction, Choi's Theorem~3, the
Hahn--Banach theorem on matrix spaces, the closedness of the separable cone and the
witness characterisation) have no counterpart in the Archive.

\section{Towards a verified HHL algorithm}\label{sec:hhl}

The HHL algorithm~\cite{hhl2009} prepares, for a Hermitian $A$ with eigen-decomposition
$A=\sum_j\lambda_j\ketbra{u_j}{u_j}$ and a unit vector $\ket b=\sum_j\beta_j\ket{u_j}$, a
state proportional to $A^{-1}\ket b$: phase estimation with the unitaries $e^{iAt}$ writes
the eigenvalues into a register, a controlled rotation of an ancilla produces the
amplitudes $C/\lambda_j$, uncomputation and a measurement of the ancilla postselect the
branch $\sum_j(\beta_j/\lambda_j)\ket{u_j}$, up to the approximation error of phase
estimation and with a success probability governed by the condition number.  Machine
checked proofs exist in Coq, in the quantum Hoare logic of Zhou, Yu and Ying
\cite{zhou2019applied,zhou2023coqq,feng2023coqqlr}; none exists in Isabelle/HOL, whose
verified algorithms are Deutsch--Jozsa, Grover and the QFT (a recent HOL Light development
treats Grover~\cite{sun2026grover}).

What a HHL proof needs, and what this project supplies in the \IMD representation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The spectral theorem.}  The whole analysis is carried out in the eigenbasis of
  $A$.  \isa{hermitian\_spectral\_decomp} provides the unitary $U$ and the real list $D$ of
  eigenvalues with $U^\dagger AU=\mathrm{diag}(D)$, together with the facts that the columns
  of $U$ are an orthonormal eigenbasis and that $\beta_j=\braket{u_j}{b}$ (Parseval).  It also
  gives the functional calculus HHL needs: $f(A)=U\,\mathrm{diag}(f(D))\,U^\dagger$ is how
  \isa{psd\_sqrt} and the pseudoinverse \isa{pinv\_list} are built, and $A^{-1}$ on the
  range of $A$ is literally the pseudoinverse whose identities $AA^+A=A$ we prove.
\item \emph{Unitarity and Hamiltonian simulation.}  $e^{iAt}=U\,\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\lambda_jt})\,U^\dagger$
  can be defined through the same decomposition; its unitarity is the lemma that a
  conjugate of a diagonal unitary by a unitary is unitary, which is a three-line
  consequence of \isa{unitary\_mult} and the characterisation of unitaries.  What is
  missing is the connection to an independently defined matrix exponential and the
  Trotter-type approximation results; for the correctness of HHL as an idealised circuit
  the spectral definition suffices.
\item \emph{Registers, tensor products, controlled operations.}  Multi-register states are
  Kronecker products; the lemmas \isa{tensor\_mat\_mult}, \isa{tensor\_mat\_adjoint},
  \isa{tensor\_mat\_unitary} and \isa{tensor\_onb} are the algebra of composite registers.
  Controlled unitaries are block matrices (\isa{four\_block\_mat}, with the block-action lemma
  \isa{block\_mat\_apply}).  The QFT itself is available from the Archive in the same
  representation~\cite{manrique2025qft}; quantum phase estimation is not yet formalised in
  Isabelle and is the largest missing circuit-level component.
\item \emph{Measurement and postselection.}  The ancilla measurement is a two-outcome
  measurement system; \isa{meas\_prob} gives the success probability and
  \isa{post\_meas\_state} the normalised output, and \isa{composite\_measurement\_prob}
  together with \isa{partial\_trace\_measurement} express that measuring the ancilla alone
  is a local measurement whose statistics are those of the reduced state.  The POVM
  formalism and the density-matrix postulates allow the same analysis for mixed inputs
  and for a noisy implementation, where the CP-map theory (Kraus representation, Choi
  matrix, convexity of CPTP) is the natural language.
\item \emph{Error analysis.}  The phase-estimation error, the dependence of the success
  probability on the condition number $\kappa$, and the final fidelity bound are
  analytic estimates that the notes do not touch; the Hilbert--Schmidt norm theory of
  \isa{Essential\_Functional\_Analysis} and the fidelity of Day~31 are starting points,
  but this part of a HHL proof would have to be built from scratch.
\end{itemize}
In short, the linear-algebraic and measurement-theoretic layer of a HHL proof---the
part that a textbook treats in one line, ``work in the eigenbasis of $A$''---is now
available and verified in the representation that the existing Isabelle quantum
libraries use.  The remaining gap is concentrated in three places: a formalisation of
quantum phase estimation on top of the QFT entry, the controlled eigenvalue-inversion
step with its amplitude bookkeeping, and the quantitative error analysis.  Each is a
project of a size comparable to one of the lecture groups above.

\section{Conclusion}

Taking a complete set of lecture notes as the specification turned out to be a productive
way to build a library: it fixes the scope, orders the work, and makes completeness
measurable.  The cost is visible in the audit: a course mentions many results it does
not prove, and a formalisation faithful to the notes must decide, item by item, what to
prove, what to restate, and what to leave.  The commentary pass that produced the
generated document is the record of those decisions; we recommend it as a deliverable for
any formalisation of a text, because it is the only place where the informal and the
formal statements are read side by side.
